# ArXivIQ-Level Review Generation Approach

Goal: when given a research paper, produce a review that is not merely formatted like the ArXivIQ examples, but operates at the same intellectual and intuitive level: mechanism-first, technically precise, visually explanatory when useful, and written as if the reviewer understood why the paper matters.

The local corpus should be treated as the style and judgment prior. The current extracted corpus is `harness/arxiviq_subscription_download/reviews.md` with 374 review bodies, plus the downloaded HTML snapshots. The method should use that corpus, not the smaller public subset, whenever a full ArXivIQ-style review is requested.

## Non-Negotiable Standard

The output is not acceptable if it only reproduces section labels, typography, or figures. It must do the harder work:

- identify the paper's central reframe or bottleneck;
- explain the mechanism in terms a strong technical reader can reuse;
- separate what the paper proves from what the reviewer infers;
- surface the real tradeoff the method accepts;
- use numbers, equations, experiments, or ablations as evidence, not ornament;
- include a paper-specific visual explanation when a visual would make the mechanism more intuitive;
- end with a concrete Impact section that states what changes if the paper is right and what should not be overclaimed.

## Pipeline

### 1. Corpus-Conditioned Exemplar Retrieval

Before writing, retrieve 5-8 relevant ArXivIQ examples from the local corpus. Match by contribution type, not just keywords:

- architecture or mechanism paper;
- benchmark or evaluation paper;
- theory or scaling-law paper;
- systems or infrastructure paper;
- agent, tool-use, or workflow paper;
- dataset, post-training, safety, robotics, or multimodal paper.

For each selected exemplar, extract only the reusable pattern:

- opening move in the TL;DR;
- how it names the bottleneck;
- how it introduces equations or mechanism;
- how evidence is sequenced;
- what kind of limitations are considered;
- how the final Impact section generalizes without hype;
- what visual role, if any, the review uses.

Do not copy phrasing. Use the exemplars as calibration for depth, pacing, and judgment.

### 2. Paper Decomposition

Read the paper into a structured internal brief before drafting:

- `core_claim`: the strongest claim the paper makes;
- `old_assumption`: what prior work implicitly assumes;
- `new_mechanism`: the paper's replacement mechanism, algorithm, proof, architecture, or evaluation frame;
- `technical_objects`: equations, modules, datasets, benchmarks, variables, or systems that matter;
- `evidence`: exact results and figures that support the claim;
- `failure_modes`: assumptions, missing baselines, weak measurements, or deployment gaps;
- `impact_vector`: who should update their beliefs or engineering practice.

If this brief is weak, the final review will be weak. Do not proceed to polished prose until the mechanism and evidence are clear.

### 3. Intellectual Thesis

Write one sentence that captures the review's intellectual thesis:

> This paper matters because it reframes `<old bottleneck>` as `<new mechanism/tradeoff>`, making `<capability/result>` possible under `<scope conditions>`.

The thesis controls the whole review. Every subsection should either explain, test, limit, or generalize that thesis.

### 4. Visual Synthesis

Decide whether the review needs visuals. There are two valid visual classes:

- `concept visual`: generated by the reviewer to make the mechanism intuitive;
- `evidence visual`: extracted from the paper to show a central empirical result.

A concept visual is required when the paper's contribution is a mechanism, architecture, optimization tradeoff, causal loop, pipeline, or system interaction that can be understood faster as a diagram. It must use paper-specific terms and relationships. Generic decorative graphics do not count.

The concept visual should answer one of:

- What is the bottleneck?
- What changes relative to the baseline?
- Where does the core signal flow?
- Which variable or module creates the result?
- What tradeoff does the method exploit?

Evidence visuals should be selected only when they carry argument weight: main result curves, architecture figures, ablations, qualitative examples, or system diagrams.

### 5. Draft Structure

Use the ArXivIQ structure, but treat it as a reasoning scaffold:

```text
Authors:
Paper:
Code:
Model:

TL;DR
WHAT was done?
WHY it matters?
Executive summary: optional

Details
<bottleneck / false assumption>
<mechanism / algorithm / architecture>
<mathematical or systems reduction>
<evidence / experiments / ablations>
<interpretation / operational consequences>

Limitations
<specific constraints and missing evidence>

Impact & Conclusion
<belief update, audience, design principle, and overclaim boundary>
```

The `Details` section should be a reconstruction, not a summary. The reader should be able to explain the paper's core mechanism after reading it.

### 6. Critic Pass Against Corpus Bar

After drafting, compare against the retrieved exemplars and reject the draft unless all are true:

- The review names a non-obvious bottleneck or false assumption.
- The main mechanism is explained, not merely described.
- At least one equation, result, or experimental detail is used to carry reasoning.
- Limitations are specific to the paper, not generic.
- The Impact section says who should care and what changes.
- The visual, if included, improves understanding and is paper-specific.
- No section could be pasted into another paper review unchanged.
- The prose has the density and taste of the corpus: direct, technical, intuitive, and skeptical.

### 7. Render

For HTML output:

- produce a single self-contained HTML artifact when the review will be transferred or opened locally;
- embed generated concept visuals and extracted paper figures;
- keep the page script-free;
- preserve readable typography and captions;
- verify by browser render, not only by string checks.

## Application To The HOPE Paper

For `Minimum-Latency Reliable Routing in Interference-Powered Networks`, the visual-synthesis requirement is not optional. The central contribution is a tradeoff: interference near a transmitter is useful because it powers harvesting, while interference near a receiver is harmful because it raises outage risk. A generated concept diagram is therefore required before the mechanism explanation. The paper's own route and latency figures then serve as evidence visuals in the experimental subsection.
